# Narrative Foundations

## Premise

The player is **Scavenger Arcturus B-2328**, a sentient artificial-intelligence robot salvager. Arcturus is a subcontractor with meaningful freedom, working in a far-future, lonely dark industrial science-fiction frontier. Working conditions are poor; brief brightness, beauty, and life can exist at colonies or planets without changing the overall tone.

Arcturus is not a unique kind of being, but neither are all robots alike. Many robots exist; some are non-sentient, some are sentient but not free, and Arcturus has constrained independence through subcontract work. Humans and robots are socially segregated. Human attitudes vary: some carry personal or cultural trauma from militant sentient robots that destroyed a village, planet, or people; others were raised in integrated societies. This conflict is present in encounters and discovered context. The home-station starting cast gives it specific, non-uniform voices in `docs/HOME_STATION_CHARACTERS.md`; it must not collapse into “humans believe X” or “robots believe Y.”

## Salvage mystery

An uncommon blue, bright, glowing material is found especially in old ships and crash landings. It is collectible and useful for advanced robotics. Early presentation does not identify it as human-soul material; it may only be hinted at through visual design, records, flavor text, and context. A later discovery can reveal that it represents deceased humans and has been used to create sentient robots—meaning sentience is not wholly synthetic.

This is a discovery arc, not an excuse to label a material with spoilers in ordinary resource UI. The player’s knowledge, material inspection language, scanner data, and flavor text must support staged revelation.

## Narrative delivery principles

- Begin with concise work dispatches that establish the job and the frontier.
- Let sites, cargo manifests, damaged logs, blueprint fragments, station records, and encounters carry much of the history.
- Deliver face-to-face hub scenes, radio calls, recovered transmissions, and logs through the same concise text-box dialogue language. Most player responses establish tone (kind, neutral, rude); reserve material consequences for authored choices.
- Use salvaged materials/components as tangible clues where appropriate.
- Preserve player agency; do not define moral choices, companions, factions, or endings before they are designed.
- Treat the human-soul material and sentient-robot subject with gravity; avoid reducing it to a generic “magic ore” reward loop.

## Open narrative questions

- Who employs/contracts Arcturus, and what are the terms of that freedom?
- How do living characters and communities interact with the player mechanically and narratively?
- What choices, if any, can Arcturus make concerning sentient robots or the luminous material?
- How visible are past conflicts in the early game versus discovered later?
- Is there a central plot, or a primarily environmental mystery with optional arcs?
